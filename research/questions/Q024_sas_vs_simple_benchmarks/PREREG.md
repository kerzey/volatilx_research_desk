## 1. Hypothesis (plain English, one sentence)

**When SAS publishes its nightly slate, those picks reach a target one ATR-distance away more often, on the same clock, than the same target distance would have been reached by the S&P 500, by an equal-weight S&P 500, by a sector-matched random pick from the same night's candidate list, or by simply ranking that same candidate list on 20-day momentum, 60-day momentum, or a plain technical strength score.**

Haci's null, in his words (INBOX 2026-09-13): *"SAS ranking contains no predictive information beyond simple benchmarks."* This question is written to be able to confirm that null. The comparison that decides is **SAS against each simple alternative** — not SAS against its own unpublished candidates, which is **Q006** and is a different question (§7).

**What "risk adjustment" means here, because it is the load-bearing choice.** Haci asked for the comparison "after risk adjustment". Every arm's target is placed at the **same multiple of that instrument's own ATR** as the SAS pick's own printed target — so SPY is asked to travel the same distance *in SPY's units of risk* that the pick is asked to travel in its own. A high-beta semiconductor name and the index are therefore not compared at the same percentage move, which would be meaningless, but at the same risk-scaled move, which is the comparison a trader sizing by ATR actually faces. Fixed-horizon risk-adjusted returns at 1 / 3 / 7 / 14 / 30 sessions — the form Haci wrote the idea in — are computed and printed in full, and **decide nothing** (rule 5, DP-01).

**Registered two-sided, per arm, with no predicted direction.** H-067 is a null hypothesis, not a directional claim. An arm on which SAS is *worse* beyond the MPE is a confirmed finding carrying that sign, and is the more consequential outcome for the platform (§9).

**One arm of Haci's list is not registered here.** Analyst revisions has no point-in-time source anywhere the desk can reach — no analyst, estimate-revision, rating-change or price-target column exists in `models.py`, in `services/symbol_context_builder.py`, in the SAS scoring layers, or in any frozen table (the only "analyst" string in `volatilx/services` is a prompt word at `services/market_intelligence_summary.py:38`). That arm is filed in `research/questions/DEFERRED.md` with the feed it needs named (DP-41, DP-47); the question proceeds with the six arms that can be built legally, and the report states the missing arm rather than substituting a proxy for it.

**One of the six is registered conditionally.** The equal-weight arm needs `RSP`, an instrument no freeze holds yet, and the availability check could not be run before lock (no Alpaca credentials in the Steward's environment; R1(d), §5). Rather than drop the arm on the strength of not knowing — which would remove a hurdle for free — it is **registered conditionally** and settled by a fixed, outcome-independent pinnability predicate at freeze-build time, written out in §3 B2 and §5 and executed before `eval.py` exists. Pinnable → `m = 6`; not pinnable → the arm is deferred **as an arm**, `m = 5`, and **no proxy is ever substituted**.

## 3. Baselines — the six simple alternatives this must beat

H-067 *is* a statement about baselines, so this section carries the substance of the question. Every arm is built from information available at 16:05 ET on the pick night, is graded by **exactly** the pick's rule (same entry basis, same clock, same "already passed at entry is not a hit" rule), and carries a target at the **same ATR distance in the same direction** as the pick it is compared against (§4). Benchmark rows are a baseline, **never sample size** (rule 6).

- **B1 — SPY (buy the index).** One instrument. Its counterpart target for pick `p` is `close_SPY,t × (1 + d_p × atr_pct_SPY,t)`, entered at SPY's own session t+1 open, graded over the same 20 sessions. SPY bars are already in the price freeze (`research/lib/freeze_prices.py:46`).
- **B2 — equal-weight S&P 500 (`RSP`) — a conditionally registered arm.** Identical construction on RSP, the tradable equal-weight S&P 500 ETF. **RSP is not in `manifest_prices_v001`**, and its availability could not be checked before lock (R1(d) could not run — no Alpaca credentials in the Steward's environment). The arm is therefore registered under a **fixed pinnability predicate**, settled once, before `manifest_prices_v002` is pinned and before `eval.py` exists, and by nothing that touches SAS, any pick or any outcome:
  > **RSP daily bars are *pinnable* iff they are retrievable from the same `data.alpaca.markets/v2/stocks/bars` endpoint, feed and adjustment basis that already serve `SPY, QQQ, IWM, SMH, DIA`, *and* the retrieved series covers every session from 2026-06-18 through `manifest_prices_v002`'s last forward-bar date with no gap.** A subscription or feed restriction, a partial series, a different adjustment basis, or any missing session all read as **not pinnable**.

  The check is the **first action of request R2** and is reported as a dated standalone line in the Steward's R2 report. **Pinnable** → RSP joins the benchmark list of `manifest_prices_v002` (and of the extension pair), EW is a registered primary, **`m = 6`**, and the schedule is unchanged (a single-instrument arm inherits SPY's computability profile, so it does not move `r_min`). **Not pinnable** → EW is deferred **as an arm** in `research/questions/DEFERRED.md` (the arm-level form of the H-067 analyst-revisions entry), **`m = 5`**, **no proxy is ever substituted** — not an equal-weight basket, not `sp500_sectors.json` constituents, not QQQ — F1's correction denominator stays at 10 (§7), and §8's question-level CONFIRMED sentence names EW among the benchmarks that did not decide. **One-way:** an arm that pinned at `manifest_prices_v002` is never dropped later; if RSP pins and its bars later prove incomplete inside the window, the arm falls to §5's below-floor rule on measured counts, not to a second look. The locked PREREG is not edited in either branch — this clause firing *is* the mechanism (rule 3).
- **B3 — sector-adjusted random.** For night `t`, take the sector composition of the SAS slate through the pinned mapping — `n_s` picks in sector ETF `s` — and draw, without replacement, `n_s` names from that night's **non-published** candidate pool in the same sector (≥ 60 prior bars required). Slots whose sector pool is empty are drawn unconstrained from the remaining pool and **counted**; picks whose own symbol is unmapped contribute an unconstrained slot and are counted. **Measured on the sealed panel (R1 §(c)):** published-slate mapping coverage **99.47%** with **BRK.B the only unmapped symbol** in the window (BF.B, the other historically unmapped name, never appears as a candidate there); candidate-pool coverage 99.86%; B3 computable on **45 of 45** eligible nights; **8 of 344 slots (2.3%)** drew unconstrained for an empty same-sector pool and 2 for an unmapped pick symbol. **1,000 draws per night**, seed 20260913, averaged within the night. Monte-Carlo draws never add to n (rule 6). The desk **never hand-maps or supplements** the pinned blob (Q022 §2.3 step 3, same reasons: it would break the sha256 pin and would be a classification rule invented after seeing which symbols failed).
- **B4 — 20-session momentum rank.** Rank that night's candidate pool by total return over sessions t−19..t from `prices_daily_split`, take the top `k_t`, ties by symbol ascending.
- **B5 — 60-session momentum rank.** Identical on sessions t−59..t.
- **B6 — simple technical rank.** The equal-weight mean of three cross-sectional percentile ranks within the same pool, each from bars dated ≤ t: (i) `C_t / SMA50_t − 1`; (ii) Wilder `RSI14_t`; (iii) the 20-session stochastic position `(C_t − min(low, 20)) / (max(high, 20) − min(low, 20))`. Top `k_t`, ties by symbol ascending. All three components are textbook, monotone "strength" measures; none is tuned, and the formula is fixed here before any outcome is read (DP-26). **This price-only composite is the registered arm, and the alternative was considered and rejected:** the platform's own `technical_structure_score` rank would make one of the six "simple alternatives" a SAS subscore, turning the arm into a layer-ablation test that belongs to H-003 (§7). That rank is kept in B7 as a descriptive comparator, printed every time and labelled "confirms nothing about H-003"; §10 threat 5 carries the cost of the choice.
- **Pool for B4–B6:** every `sas_candidates` row on night `t` with ≥ 60 prior daily bars, **including the published picks themselves** — a naive rank is allowed to choose the same names, and if it chooses all of them the answer to H-067 is "SAS is momentum", which is exactly what the question is for. The overlap share (picks in common between the SAS slate and each rank arm) is a **composition count, not an outcome**, and is printed per night and in aggregate (§4 secondaries). The alternative — removing the published slate from the pool first — was rejected: it would manufacture a difference by forbidding the benchmark from choosing the best names, and H-067's wording is "would a naive rank of the same list have done as well". On the sealed panel the pool ran **min 56 / median 63 / max 68** names with ≥ 60 prior bars and was never smaller than that night's `k_t`, on either the inclusive or the non-published-only reading (R1 §(b)).
- **B7 — descriptive comparators, never a verdict:** QQQ, IWM and SMH on the B1 construction (already in the freeze, free to compute); the equal-weight **candidate pool** (all non-published candidates, unmatched — this is Q006's B2 and is cited as such); a rank arm built on the platform's own `technical_structure_score` (one SAS layer instead of seven — **this belongs to H-003, layer ablation, and confirms nothing about it**, §7); and a random draw with no sector adjustment.
- **Permutation null:** paired sign-flip on the night-level arm differences (§4), drawn **jointly across arms** so the cross-arm correlation is preserved for the step-down statistic — 10,000 draws, seed 20260913.

## 4. Objective metric (rule 5 — price path, measured the way it is traded)

**Entry basis: the official 09:30 ET open of session t+1** (DP-03(b), DP-42), for the picks and for every arm alike — one basis on both sides. The decision this question informs is taken at publication and acted on at the next open. The pick-night close basis (DP-11's construction) is computed and printed as a named sensitivity and decides nothing.

**Primary level: L3** — `public_payload_json.lane_plans.swing_trading.targets[0]`, the first swing target (DP-42: the hypothesis names no level, so the swing-lane default applies). **Primary clock: first touch within sessions t+1..t+20** (DP-09; the platform's own 40-session swing window, `services/sas_conviction_card.py:194`, is printed alongside descriptively wherever matured).

**The distance, which is the whole comparison.** For pick `p` on night `t`: `d_p = (L3_p − C_{t,p}) / ATR_p`, with ATR14 computed from `prices_daily_split` bars dated ≤ t. Every arm counterpart `m` is graded on a target at that same distance in its own units: `target_{m,p} = C_{t,m} × (1 + d_p × atr_pct_m)`.

**Touch rules, applied by `eval.py` with no judgment:**
1. `HIT = 1` iff some session `s ∈ [t+1, t+20]` has `high_s ≥ target`.
2. **A target already at or through the session t+1 open is not a hit** (DP-03, DP-26): graded `HIT = 0`, flagged `passed_at_entry`, and the primaries are recomputed with those rows removed from both sides as a named sensitivity. The identical rule applies to every arm.
3. A gap through the level during t+1..t+20 is a touch.
4. No stop is assumed anywhere and no touch is an exit (rule 5, DP-02). Adverse excursion and counter-direction touches are reported, never used as exits.

**Cross-product grading — how an arm is scored at the pick's distance.** For arm `a` on night `t`, the arm's rate at distance `d_p` is the mean of `HIT` over the arm's members graded at `d_p` (for B1/B2 the arm has one member, so the mean is that instrument's own indicator). This removes any need to pair an arm member to a particular pick, which would otherwise be an arbitrary choice inside the estimator. Then:

- `D_{a,t} = mean_p[ HIT_p ] − mean_p[ rate_a(d_p) ]`, in percentage points — **one number per night per arm, and the night is the unit of inference** (rule 6).

**Primary endpoints (six, pre-specified, one per arm, all two-sided):**

> **E_a = mean over contributing nights of `D_{a,t}`**, for `a ∈ {SPY, EW, SECRAND, MOM20, MOM60, TECH}` — **EW only if it is registered** (§3 B2's pinnability predicate; `m` is 6 or 5 and is settled before `eval.py` exists).

**Question-level statistic (no new endpoint, a roll-up of the registered arms):** `E_min = min_a E_a` — SAS's excess over the *best* simple alternative. H-067 asks whether SAS carries information *beyond* simple benchmarks, plural, so the question-level verdict turns on `E_min` and not on the friendliest arm (§8). This choice is DP-45 applied: it is the reading least likely to reach CONFIRMED.

**Two multiplicity corrections, both printed, the stricter governing.** Every primary prints three numbers side by side: the **raw** sign-flip p; the **Benjamini–Hochberg q** across F1's full registered primary set (§7); and the **step-down max-T adjusted p** across this question's registered arms, computed on the same joint sign-flip draws so the cross-arm correlation is preserved. The two answer different questions — BH is rule 8's family false-discovery control, max-T is the family-wise control across correlated arms — and **neither replaces the other**: an arm is CONFIRMED only if it clears **both** thresholds (§8), and `E_min` carries the max-T adjusted p of the arm attaining the minimum. A max-T adjusted p is never reported as a q, or the reverse.

**The SAS arm's absolute touch rate never travels alone.** An absolute L3-touch rate for the published slate is descriptive on its own (rule 5: a hit rate without a distance-matched control is description), and is **never printed, quoted or ledgered without B3 — the same-night, distance-matched, sector-adjusted unpublished draw — beside it in the same table**. Only the `E_a` differences carry a verdict.

**Clause-7 money gate (a gate, not a primary — §8).** The platform's own committed SELECT scale-out `[0, 15, 30, 15, 25, 15]` on the flattened L1–L6 ladder (`services/sas_conviction_card.py:203-218`), **truncated to a 20-session budget** so the gate shares the primary's clock and maturity: each level is graded on its own lane window capped at 20 sessions from the entry, and any unfilled weight is marked out at the session-20 close. The result is expressed in **ATR per trade** (DP-10's unit, which is the risk adjustment in Haci's own units), and each arm counterpart carries synthetic levels at the identical six ATR distances. The 60-session version of the same plan (each level on its true 20/40/60 lane window) is printed descriptively wherever matured. **Cost of the truncation, stated plainly:** 40% of the plan's weight sits on L5/L6, whose own lane window exceeds the budget, so the gate leans partly on the session-20 mark — identically on both sides of every contrast.

**Registered secondaries — printed always, deciding never:**

- **The fixed-horizon panel Haci asked for:** mean return at **1 / 3 / 7 / 14 / 30 sessions** from the t+1 open, per arm, raw and ATR-normalized (`(P_{t+k} − entry) / ATR`), with the night-clustered information ratio of the SAS-minus-arm difference. Printed where matured, counts shown. **Fixed-horizon return is descriptive by rule 5 and DP-01 and never decides, at any point, in any branch.**
- The rest of the ladder: L1, L2, L4, L5, L6 on their own lane windows, the counter-direction levels and the printed swing stop (recomputed from bars, never read from the outcome table), the order in which levels were hit, MFE and MAE in ATR, sessions-to-first-touch (descriptive by DP-44).
- **The distance profile:** each arm's touch rate as a function of `d`, and E_a within terciles of `d_p`. This is the read on §10 threat 1 and can never be promoted to primary.
- Overlap composition: picks in common between the SAS slate and each rank arm, per night and pooled.
- B7's descriptive comparators (§3); the platform close-basis rates; the 40-session L3 result wherever matured.
- Sub-cells: score band (80–85 / 85–90 / 90+), `best_timeframe` lane, `confidence_level`, `k_t`, top-3 by `selected_rank` vs the rest, tape stratum, regime label — **reported only at ≥ 20 measured contributing nights, SUPPRESSED below, counts printed either way**. **Pre-suppressed at lock (§5), permanently, counts only: the 90+ score band** (projected 9.3 contributing nights at 142 sessions), **`k_t` = 4** (3.1) and **`k_t` = 3** (3.1), from R1's projection table. Two cells are **marginal and are deliberately not pre-suppressed** — `k_t` = 7 and regime `bullish`, both projected 21.6 — and fall to the standing ≥ 20-**measured**-night rule at the pass. Every tape stratum, and every regime label other than `strongly_bullish` / `bullish`, likewise falls to that rule: the sealed panel carries **zero** observations of them, so there is no rate to project and a pre-suppression there would be an invented number.

**What SUPPRESSED means, fixed at lock.** Counts only — no point estimate, no sign, no "small n, directionally", no q-value. A cell suppressed at lock stays suppressed even if it clears 20 measured nights at the pass. Suppression restricts **affirmative reporting only**: it never removes a cell from §8 clause 6, and never by itself makes an endpoint INCONCLUSIVE.

**Quotability.** The primary is a 20-session window and is therefore **NON_QUOTABLE** under rule 12 in every branch, and nothing is quotable at all before PROSPECTIVELY_CONFIRMED (rule 10).

**Inference (rule 6).** CI: stationary block bootstrap over the ordered contributing nights, **expected block length 10 sessions** (half the forward window — the Q011 / Q015 / Q022 convention, fixed here and not chosen after seeing results), 2,000 resamples; the date-clustered CI is printed alongside and the **block-bootstrap interval decides**. p-value: paired sign-flip permutation, 10,000 draws, seed 20260913, joint across arms. Every estimate prints n(contributing nights, per arm), n(nights dated after the lock commit), n(eligible picks), n(HIT), n(`passed_at_entry`), n(arm rows), n(unconstrained sector slots), n(ungradeable), n(immature), n(excluded by reason), the mapping coverage, and the rank-arm overlap share.

## 5. Sample floors and expected n

- **Floors (rule 6 as read by DP-21):** ≥ **80 contributing nights per primary endpoint** — that is, **per arm**, since an arm short of the floor cannot decide and leaves the deciding set (the demotion bullet below says what happens to it) — ≥ **20 contributing nights per reported sub-cell**, and ≥ **30 contributing nights dated after the lock commit** (DP-24). ## 8. Decision rule (numeric, written before unsealing)

Each `E_a` is in **percentage points of L3-touch rate within 20 sessions, SAS minus arm `a`**, over that arm's contributing nights. **MPE = ±5.0 pp** for every arm — DP-20's standing number for a control-adjusted or matched touch-rate endpoint, applied unchanged (DP-44). `E_a` is a **single** difference of two matched touch rates, structurally the Q006 E1 shape, not a difference of differences, so the doubling clause does not apply and the MPE is not raised. **The MPE is never lowered at the decision pass, in any branch.** Every endpoint is two-sided and every verdict carries its sign.

**Per-arm verdict (the primary output — six of them, five if the EW arm is not registered, each ledgered):**

- **HISTORICALLY_CONFIRMED** for arm `a` requires **all** of:
  1. **≥ 80 contributing nights for that arm**, **≥ 30 of them after the lock commit** (DP-21, DP-24), plus ≥ 20 contributing nights for any sub-cell that is reported (below floor → SUPPRESSED, counts only, which does not by itself make the endpoint INCONCLUSIVE);
  2. `|E_a| > 5.0 pp`;
  3. the **block-bootstrap** 95% CI (§4) excludes 0;
  4. **both multiplicity thresholds, each on its own statistic, both printed:** BH **q ≤ 0.10** across **F1's 10 primaries** (§7), computed on the sign-flip permutation p, **and** the **step-down max-T adjusted p ≤ 0.10** across this question's registered arms. Neither substitutes for the other and an adjusted p is never restated as a q;
  5. the point estimate has the **same sign in both halves** (§6), neither half beyond MPE in the opposite sign;
  6. no tape stratum and no regime cell with ≥ 20 contributing nights beyond MPE in the opposite sign;
  7. **the clause-7 money gate: the same-arm plan contrast (§4, ATR per trade, SAS minus arm) has the same sign as `E_a`** — point estimate only, no CI requirement. A pick that reaches its target more often but pays less than the benchmark per unit of risk is not an edge, and the desk will not license one.
- **NULL** for arm `a`: floors met **and** the 95% CI includes 0 **and** `|E_a| < 5.0 pp`. **"SAS reaches its own target no more often than [SPY / equal-weight / a sector-matched random pick / a momentum rank] would have at the same risk-scaled distance" is a real finding**, is Haci's own H0, and is ledgered with exactly the care of a positive.
- **INCONCLUSIVE** for arm `a`: anything else — halves disagreeing in sign; a tape or regime cell beyond MPE in the opposite sign; the clause-7 gate disagreeing in sign; `0 < |E_a| ≤ 5.0 pp` with the CI excluding 0 ("real but below MPE", rule 6); or the CI including 0 with `|E_a| ≥ 5.0 pp`. **A floor shortfall is not INCONCLUSIVE:** it fires the single DP-13 extension; if the arm is still short after it, the arm **leaves the deciding set** — out of `m`, out of BH, out of `E_min` — is recorded in `DEFERRED.md` at arm level with its measured counts, and is **still built and printed descriptively wherever it reaches ≥ 20 contributing nights**, flagged "did not decide" and named in §8's question-level sentence (§5).

**Question-level verdict (H-067 itself):**

- **CONFIRMED (SAS carries information beyond simple benchmarks):** **every** registered arm satisfies its full clause list 1–7 above with a positive sign, **and** `E_min > +5.0 pp` with the **step-down max-T adjusted p ≤ 0.10** for the arm attaining the minimum, and no arm is CONFIRMED-negative. Beating five alternatives and losing to the sixth is not a confirmation of H-067; it is a description of what SAS is, and is reported as such. **The CONFIRMED sentence names every benchmark that did not decide** — an arm removed from the deciding set for a floor shortfall (§5), and the EW arm if RSP did not pin — so "beats every simple alternative" is never said over an arm that was quietly not tested.
- **NULL (Haci's H0 stands):** no arm is CONFIRMED-positive **and** at least four arms are NULL with floors met. The headline sentence is then "the published slate did not outreach simple alternatives at the same risk-scaled distance over this window", with the per-arm numbers beside it.
- **CONFIRMED-NEGATIVE:** any arm CONFIRMED with `E_a < −5.0 pp`. This is a finding about the platform, not a failure of the study, and §9 says what follows from it.
- **INCONCLUSIVE:** anything else, including the common and expected case of a mixed per-arm table.
- **What a confirmed arm does and does not mean (binding on the report).** `E_SPY > 0` means **"over this window, a published pick reached a target one ATR-distance away, within 20 sessions of the next open, more often than SPY reached the same distance in SPY's own ATR units"**. It does **not** mean the picks outperformed the index in dollars, that a portfolio of picks beat a portfolio of SPY, or that the difference survives costs — no stop, no position sizing and no transaction cost exists anywhere in this question (DP-02), and the fixed-horizon return panel never decides. The index arms in particular speak for **one tape**: §10 threat 2 is quoted in the report next to their numbers, not in a footnote. **Every verdict sentence also carries its population scope: bullish published picks only, with the measured bearish share of the slate printed beside it** (3.7% on the sealed panel, re-measured in-window — §2.2). And **the SAS arm's absolute touch rate is never stated anywhere without B3 beside it** (§4); only the `E_a` differences carry a verdict.
- **PROSPECTIVELY_CONFIRMED:** reachable from this run by design (§5, §6) — the whole window post-dates the lock commit, so an arm meeting the HISTORICALLY_CONFIRMED clauses on ≥ 30 post-lock contributing nights under the unmodified `eval.py` reaches it directly. The clause does not weaken. No subscriber-facing statement before that (rule 10), and even then the basis is NON_QUOTABLE (rule 12).

## 10. Known threats to validity (registrar's own list)

1. **A touch rate at a fixed ATR multiple is not perfectly scale-free across instruments.** An index and a single stock with the same ATR-normalized target do not have the same probability of reaching it: the index has thinner tails per unit of ATR and more persistent drift, the single stock the reverse. ATR normalization equalizes scale, not shape. The **distance profile** (§4: each arm's touch rate as a function of `d`, and E_a within terciles of `d_p`) is the registered read on this, and it is descriptive by design — it can explain a result, never rescue one. This threat is the reason the index arms are reported with their shape statistics attached.
2. **The index arms are, statistically, a handful of observations.** SPY has one path; **142 elapsed sessions** of 20-session forward windows over about seven months contain perhaps seven genuinely independent index windows. The block bootstrap (10-session blocks) narrows nothing that is not there, and it is optimistic. Consequently `E_SPY` and `E_EW` are claims about **one tape**, rule 7's "a result that only holds in one period is not a result" bites hardest here, and §8 requires the same sign in both halves and in every tape stratum with ≥ 20 nights. **This is measured, not hypothetical:** all 26 matured sealed nights carried a legal same-evening `v1.2` label and every one of them was `strongly_bullish` (19) or `bullish` (7), with zero nights of any other value (R1). If the prospective window repeats that, "holds across regimes" is **structurally untestable rather than under-measured**, the honest verdict on the index arms is INCONCLUSIVE, the report says so in those words beside the numbers, and the absence of a contradicting regime cell is never read as robustness (§6).
3. **The rank arms are drawn from SAS's own candidate pool, so they are not "the market".** They test whether the *ranking* adds information beyond a naive rank of the same names — which is exactly H-067's wording — and they cannot speak to whether the screen that built the pool adds information. That second question is not asked here and the report must not imply it was.
4. **B4–B6 are correlated with one another and with the picks.** Momentum-20, momentum-60 and a technical strength rank will often choose overlapping names, and SAS picks are momentum names. BH treats correlated tests conservatively, and the overlap share is printed; but a near-total overlap makes `E_a ≈ 0` **by construction**, and that reading — "SAS is momentum" — must be stated from the overlap counts, not inferred from a null alone.
5. **The invented technical rank is a registrar's convention, not a standard.** Three textbook components, equal weights, no tuning, fixed before any outcome is read (DP-26) — but a different plain technical rank would give a different arm. §3 B6 records the alternative that was considered and why it was rejected (it would have made one of the six "simple alternatives" a SAS subscore); the report states that B6 is one reasonable simple rank and not *the* simple rank, and prints the platform's own `technical_structure_score` rank beside it as a descriptive comparator that confirms nothing about H-003.
6. **L3 is where the platform prints it, not where volatility says it should be.** PI-009 found the ladder is distance, not forecast, and DATA_NOTES records a price-scale defect for some symbols (handled by §2.4's screen) and a ladder-monotonicity mechanism change (the window sits entirely after it). Because every arm inherits the *same* distance, a badly placed L3 is placed equally badly on both sides — that is the design — but if distance itself correlates with pick quality, `E_a` mixes selection with target placement. The distance-tercile decomposition is the registered read.
7. **Bearish picks are excluded, so the verdict speaks for the bullish book only.** The cost is **measured, not caveated: 3.7% of the sealed-panel published slate — 14 of 374 rows, Jul 4.9% / Aug 1.2% / Sep 8.2%, zero `mixed`** (R1 §(e)) — and is re-measured per month in-window and printed (§2.2). It is small in a strong tape and would not be in a weak one, which is precisely when the exclusion would matter most; §8's claim-scope sentence carries it either way.
8. **A publication-path ship inside the window changes the SAS arm itself.** The window is placed after every known population-defining ship, and the **DP-50(a)/(b) sweep at lock came back clean** — `2d5776c` (PI-001, `uoa_symbol_daily`, already a banned input here) and `22a2e1b` (EN-002, flag-off internal tool), plus two merges, none touching the publication predicate, floor, slate size, weights, timeframe multipliers, lane-plan writer or ladder (R1 §(f)) — so no window split is required at lock. But the window is open for eight months on a shared database (DP-50), so a new ship — a floor change, a slate-size change, a predicate change — would split the question at the ship date (DP-50(a)) or move the start and recompute the schedule (§5). R2 repeats the sweep for the in-window period and catches such a ship by its timestamp, rather than the desk discovering it at the pass.
9. **RSP is a proxy for "equal-weight S&P 500", not the construction itself.** It carries a management fee, a quarterly rebalance and its own tracking behaviour. The alternative — building the index from constituents — needs ~500 symbols of price history the desk does not hold and a point-in-time constituent list it does not have either (§3 B2). The proxy is named as a proxy in the report. **And the arm may not exist at all:** its registration turns on a pinnability check that could not be run before lock (§3 B2, §5 R1(d)); if RSP does not pin, `m` falls to 5, no substitute is built, and §8's CONFIRMED sentence names EW among the arms that did not decide.
10. **Six arms (five if RSP does not pin) on one population means six correlated tests on the same nights.** BH controls the false-discovery rate across F1's ten primaries and the step-down max-T controls the family-wise error across the arms; both are printed and both must be cleared, but neither makes an underpowered arm informative. INCONCLUSIVE on some arms is a likely outcome, is the acceptable direction of error, and is not evidence of absence.
11. **The Monte-Carlo sector draw has its own variance.** 1,000 draws per night with a fixed seed makes it reproducible, not noiseless; the draw-to-draw standard error of the night-level B3 rate is printed beside every B3 figure, and B3 rows never add to n (rule 6).
12. **The fixed-horizon panel is the part a reader will most want to act on, and it decides nothing.** Rule 5 and DP-01 forbid it deciding; §9 forbids any rule resting on it. The report states both facts in the same paragraph as the numbers.
13. **The ETF arms are graded on split-adjusted bars with no dividends, and that bias runs in the hypothesis's favour.** B1, B2 and B7's ETF comparators use **price bars only**, the same basis as every stock arm — one basis on both sides (rule 5). ETF distributions run roughly 1.3–1.6% a year, i.e. ~0.10–0.13% over a 20-session window, a few percent of a typical index ATR-distance, so the index arms are graded slightly **low** and `E_SPY` / `E_EW` are slightly **overstated in SAS's favour**. The effect is small but it is not zero and it is not symmetric, so it is printed **beside the arm table, not in a footnote**: a confirmation on an index arm that sits within a few tenths of a percentage point of MPE should be read with this bias in mind.
