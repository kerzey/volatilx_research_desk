- [ ] **A sudden burst of option premium in a name, on very short expiry, points the way the underlying then moves — and the burst, not the live tab's ratio, is the thing to measure** (Haci, 2026-09-17/18, from watching the UOA live tab: NFLX showed unusual *put buying* and HOOD unusual *call buying*, both ~1-DTE; next session NFLX fell and HOOD rose, both option lines multiplied — Haci held the NFLX puts, +1285%). The claim in one line: a burst that is large relative to the name's ordinary rate of option premium, concentrated in near-dated contracts, with a clear buy side, is followed by a move in that direction in the underlying within one to two sessions. **Measure the burst, not the level.** The live tab's `unusual_ratio` is cumulative-to-date premium over `trailing-20 mean daily premium × fraction of session elapsed` (`services/uoa_widenet_rules.py:216-228`, called `routers/uoa_live.py:232`). If a burst of excess premium `B` lands at time `t` and flow proceeds at the ordinary rate `r` afterwards, the reading is `1 + B/(r·t)` — so **the "4× then it calmed down as new activity came in" that Haci watched is what the formula does by construction, not the market absorbing anything.** `B` is the invariant; the ratio is a time-of-day-confounded encoding of it that decays hyperbolically whatever the tape does. Candidate burst measures for the PREREG to choose between: per-5-minute-bucket premium over the name's own trailing per-bucket rate; the largest single-bucket jump of the session; signed by `buy_premium − sell_premium` per `option_type`. Endpoint: rule 5 path metrics on the **underlying**, entry at the tick after the burst is visible on the tab (a target already passed at that entry is not a hit), against a distance-matched control of same-session non-bursting names. **This observation is only meaningful because EN-020 Phase A shipped 2026-09-15:** before the first covered session the "buy" on that tab was PI-023's closing-quote artefact, which tracks the day's drift (ρ −0.57); from 2026-09-16 it is the quote bracketing the print.

- [ ] **Very short expiry is the part of Haci's observation that is actually new, so test it as its own arm:** is a burst concentrated in contracts expiring within one or two sessions more informative than the same-sized burst in 30–45 DTE lines? Mechanism for real: a near-dated contract is nearly all extrinsic decay, so paying up in size for one is a dated, expensive bet that needs the move immediately — it is the least ambiguous thing a flow reader can see, and it cannot be a slow accumulation or a rolled hedge. Mechanism for noise: 0–2 DTE is also where dealer hedging, index-adjacent flow and pure lottery buying live, and the standing desk finding "short DTE underperforms" points the other way — though that finding is about the **lane DTE recommended for a SAS pick**, not about a burst in the UOA tape, so it is a prior worth stating, not an answer. `uoa_contract_intraday` carries `expiration_date` and `strike_price` on every bucket row (`models.py:1087-1088`), so the DTE stratification needs no new data.
- [ ] **Does the easing carry information, or is it only arithmetic?** (Haci, 2026-09-17: "then option activity averaged down… it eased the spike"). Two rival readings, and the desk can separate them because the per-bucket series is stored: (a) the decay is the denominator growing and the burst is over — the *later* buckets simply contain ordinary flow; (b) the decay is real absorption — the burst was met by size on the other side, and a burst that is *not* followed by continued one-sided buying is a different animal from one that is. Test: after removing the mechanical `1 + B/(r·t)` component, does the residual — continued net one-sided premium in the buckets after the burst, or its absence — add anything to the direction call beyond `B` alone? Also the time-of-day arm: an early-session burst and a late-session burst read the same on the tab at different absolute sizes, so measure whether the hour it lands changes what follows.
- [ ] **The live tab's ratio has three measurement problems and Haci's 4× may be partly one of them** (desk, 2026-09-18, reading `routers/uoa_live.py` after Haci's question — file as platform issues or an enhancement, whichever fits). (a) **Pre-D0 baseline.** The denominator is the trailing-20 mean of `uoa_symbol_daily.total_premium` (`:166-183`). PI-020's D0 is **2026-09-15**, so on 2026-09-17 roughly 19 of those 20 sessions are truncated-to-one-page nights — understated for exactly the most liquid names, and NFLX is one of them. An understated denominator **inflates the ratio**: some of that 4× may be the baseline, not the burst. The tab is on a mixed baseline until ~2026-10-14. (b) **Numerator and denominator are not the same quantity:** the numerator sums only *classified* (`buy + sell`) premium (`:118`, `:232`) while the denominator is trailing mean *total* premium, classified or not — so any unknown share deflates the ratio, and it moves with coverage rather than with flow. (c) **The 30- and 60-minute window views look broken:** the bucket query is filtered to the window (`:100-105`) but `elapsed` is still measured from the session open (`:98`), so a windowed row divides one window's premium by a full-session-elapsed denominator, and decays through the day for that reason alone. The fix Haci would actually use is to show the burst beside the level — per-bucket premium against the name's own per-bucket rate — rather than a single cumulative number that falls all afternoon no matter what the flow does.
